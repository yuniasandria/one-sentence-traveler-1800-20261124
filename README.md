# One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800

[![make.fun](https://img.shields.io/badge/make.fun-bounty-blue)](https://make.fun/bounty/u-po-pu-one-sentence-for-a-traveler-from-1800-7e6162fb)
[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-yellow.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)
[![Python 3.13](https://img.shields.io/badge/python-3.13-blue.svg)](https://www.python.org/)

One sentence, at most 200 characters, that explains the modern internet to a
time traveler who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — using only words a
person from 1800 could already know. No words coined after their time, no list
of features, just one picture they could hold in their head.

Built with [make.fun](https://make.fun) — referral code `6cmdpsm5`.

## The sentence

> It is a road of wires joining town to town across the earth, where letters, pictures, and voices fly faster than any horse, so a man in London may speak with a friend in Canton while his tea cools.

197 characters. One road carries the whole picture: wires join town to town
(the connection itself), letters, pictures, and voices are the cargo (messages,
images, speech — the internet's three jobs, in words an 1800 reader owns), and
they fly faster than any horse (instant, worldwide delivery without naming any
modern mechanism). The close is the image that stays: a man in London speaking
with a friend in Canton while his tea cools.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the sentence to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or the
first argument as the output directory). Python 3.13, standard library only.
